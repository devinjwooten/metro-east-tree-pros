# Content Style Guide

## Audience and voice

Write for homeowners, property managers, and business owners who need a clear
next step. The voice is calm, practical, local, and professional. Explain risk
without alarmism and make the service process feel manageable.

## Writing rules

- Lead with outcomes: safety, access, healthy trees, usable space, or a cleaner
  property.
- Use concrete language such as “branches over a driveway” rather than vague
  claims such as “industry-leading solutions.”
- Keep paragraphs short and use headings, bullets, and FAQ answers to support
  scanning.
- Use “we” for Metro East Tree Pros and “you” for the customer, without
  overusing either.
- Use plain language first; define technical terms only if they help a customer
  decide what to do.
- Avoid unsupported claims, fake scarcity, price promises, fabricated local
  details, or guarantees that have not been approved.

## Service-page narrative

A strong service page generally follows this order:

1. What the service helps solve.
2. Why professional help matters.
3. Common signs or use cases.
4. What the team does and what customers can expect.
5. Process, cleanup, safety, and trust information.
6. Relevant FAQs.
7. A specific estimate or call CTA.

## Local references

Use Metro East and city names naturally to explain service coverage. Do not turn
the body copy into a city list. City names must be spelled consistently:
Belleville, O'Fallon, Edwardsville, Collinsville, Fairview Heights, Swansea,
Shiloh, Columbia, and Waterloo.

## Reviews and proof

Only publish verified reviews with permission and accurate attribution. Until
then, placeholder testimonials must visibly say they are sample content. Never
pair sample reviews with review structured data or star-rating claims.

## Emergency copy

For life-threatening situations, downed power lines, or immediate danger,
instruct visitors to call 911 and their utility provider first. Do not promise
arrival times or insurance outcomes.
