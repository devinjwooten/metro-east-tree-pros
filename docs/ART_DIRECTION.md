# Metro East Tree Pros Art Direction

## Purpose and Authority

This document is the visual-production standard for every image generated for
Metro East Tree Pros. It applies to hero imagery, service imagery, gallery
visuals, supporting editorial images, and future city-page artwork.

Read this document with [AGENTS.md](./AGENTS.md), [VISION.md](./VISION.md),
and [IMAGE_STRATEGY.md](./IMAGE_STRATEGY.md) before creating or assigning an
image. Where the documents overlap, use the stricter standard. This guide
governs art direction; it does not authorize unsupported business claims,
unverified local project claims, or unsafe depictions.

The desired result is a cohesive visual campaign that makes a visitor think:
“These people look capable, careful, local, and easy to trust.” The work must
feel like authentic, premium Midwestern service photography—not generic stock
imagery, a dramatic disaster scene, or an artificial illustration.

## Creative Brief

The campaign should consistently feel premium, trustworthy, clean, modern, and
appropriate for high-end residential properties. Each scene should tell a
specific, believable service story rather than presenting a generic worker in
front of a tree. People remember a clear arrival, careful removal, thoughtful
consultation, or satisfying cleanup more readily than a context-free work
scene.

## Core Visual Character

Every approved image should feel:

- Photorealistic, documentary, and grounded in a plausible Metro East setting.
- Calm, capable, clean, and professionally prepared.
- Bright and natural, with summer or early-fall daylight as the default.
- Premium but approachable: a real local service company, never a luxury
  landscaping advertisement or a national-franchise stock campaign.
- Consistent with the site’s deep green, slate, white, and natural wood/soil
  color palette.

Photography should use warm natural color, sharp focus, believable modern homes,
professional crews, and realistic safety equipment. A scene may have quiet
space for web copy, but it must never feel empty or disconnected from the
service story.

Prioritize a sense of organized expertise over spectacle. A tidy work zone, a
properly equipped crew, a healthy lawn, and clear communication are stronger
trust signals than exaggerated action.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Every major page has one unique hero image. Do not reuse a hero image,
   near-duplicate composition, house, crew arrangement, truck angle, or tree
   between major pages.
2. Never embed text, phone numbers, ratings, watermarks, badges, calls to
   action, or prominent logos in generated images. The website supplies all
   text and branding accessibly in HTML.
3. Never portray a generated image as a verified Metro East Tree Pros job,
   employee, customer, review, city landmark, or completed local project.
4. Show correct safety practices. If a scene cannot be rendered credibly and
   safely, choose a less hazardous moment in the workflow.
5. Do not use an image merely because it is attractive. Its subject must help
   a visitor understand the page’s service, company story, or next step.
6. Every image must leave enough uncluttered space for its intended crop and
   surrounding page copy.
7. Inspect the completed asset before use. Reject visible anatomy errors,
   impossible rigging, malformed equipment, unsafe work methods, unreadable
   pseudo-text, duplicate faces, or a synthetic “stock-photo” appearance.
8. Never use cartoon, illustrated, CGI-looking, blurred, or otherwise
   artificial-looking visuals. Bark, tree scale, chainsaws, climbing gear, and
   work methods must withstand close visual inspection.

## Campaign Baseline

### Setting

Use believable Metro East Illinois residential and commercial environments:

- Established suburban homes with mature deciduous trees, driveways, lawns,
  and modest landscaping.
- Light commercial sites, offices, apartment communities, or managed grounds
  when the story calls for commercial work.
- Natural Midwest tree species and seasonal details. Avoid coastal palms,
  mountain landscapes, tropical vegetation, implausibly huge lawns, or
  architecturally unrelated homes.

An image may be inspired by the region but must not claim a specific city.
Future city-page visuals need a distinct composition and should be described
generically unless the business supplies a verified factual connection.

### Lighting and Color

- Default to bright, clean natural daylight or soft, lightly overcast light.
- Use early-fall warmth sparingly; green foliage and natural bark/soil tones
  should remain believable.
- Maintain neutral whites and clean green crew apparel. Avoid teal-orange
  blockbuster grading, heavy HDR, tinted shadows, crushed blacks, or neon
  greens.
- Emergency imagery may be moodier, but it must remain clear enough to convey
  safety, control, and readable web overlays. Do not make it night-dark unless
  night operations are an approved, truthful service claim.

### People, Apparel, and Equipment

People should look like experienced local professionals, not fashion models.
Use a small crew where the work benefits from it; do not populate a simple job
with unnecessary people.

- Crew members wear coordinated deep-green work shirts, work pants, practical
  boots, and clean white hard hats when task-appropriate.
- A small, subtle Metro East Tree Pros mark is permitted only if it looks
  realistic. It must never be oversized, distorted, or relied upon as a logo
  asset.
- Use realistic trucks, chippers, stump grinders, ropes, harnesses, cones, and
  protective equipment. Trucks and equipment should be visually consistent
  across the campaign: modern, clean, practical, and similarly maintained.
  Avoid prominent vehicle branding and impossible tool configurations.
- Keep equipment organized. Cones, rope zones, and tool placement should read
  as deliberate rather than decorative clutter.
- Homeowners, when present, should be natural and respectfully dressed. Use
  them for communication or completion moments—not staged celebration.

## Safety Direction

Safety is both a brand principle and a visual requirement. Never generate:

- A person beneath a suspended limb, cut branch, elevated load, or active saw.
- An arborist without credible fall protection when working aloft.
- A chainsaw operator without appropriate PPE or a controlled stance.
- Workers touching, cutting near, or casually standing under utility lines.
- A climber on an implausibly thin limb, ladder, or unsafe roof position.
- Chaotic storm scenes, injured people, property destruction, or fear-based
  depictions.

For complex removals, show the preparation, the controlled lowering of a
section, a ground crew in a clear zone, or the cleanup phase. When in doubt,
favor a composed, safety-first work moment over a dramatic action shot.

## Hero-Image Composition

Hero sources should be wide landscape images, ideally at least 2400 × 1350
pixels. They must survive a wide desktop crop and a tall mobile crop.

- Reserve approximately 35–45% quiet space for the headline and CTAs.
- Place visually busy work, faces, trucks, and high-detail equipment away from
  the initial text-safe zone.
- Create depth: foreground lawn, driveway, or work area; a clear subject in the
  middle ground; and a natural tree, home, or sky backdrop.
- Use a single unmistakable story. The image should read at thumbnail size.
- Vary camera height, viewpoint, property type, foliage, crew activity, and
  focal placement across pages.
- Ensure the mobile center crop retains either the subject or a calm readable
  field; do not make a desktop-only composition that becomes meaningless on a
  phone.

The standard hero overlay is supplied by page code. Do not darken, blur, or
add a gradient inside the source image to force legibility. The source should
remain useful for future contexts.

## Page-Specific Direction

| Page | Visual story | Composition guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homepage | A polished crew arriving at a beautiful, well-kept suburban home. | Show organized equipment and a welcoming, capable arrival. Keep substantial open lawn or tree canopy for copy. |
| Tree Removal | A crane lifting a large trunk section over a roof during a controlled removal. | Show credible rigging, safe clearance, a protected work area, and a safely positioned crew. Avoid sensational destruction or anyone beneath the load. |
| Tree Trimming | An arborist high in a healthy mature canopy, carefully pruning. | Emphasize correct harness use, deliberate canopy management, and a protected work area rather than aggressive cutting. |
| Stump Grinding | A professional grinder removing a medium stump. | Show realistic wood chips, a tidy lawn edge, safe operator posture, and controlled debris. |
| Emergency Tree Service | Crew clearing a storm-damaged tree after heavy winds. | Communicate calm response, a secure perimeter, and restored access—not panic, active utility hazards, or dangerous work. |
| Storm Cleanup | Organized removal and cleanup after a storm. | Highlight restored access, collected debris, and a clearly progressing cleanup operation. |
| About | Team talking with a homeowner after completing a job. | Favor relaxed, natural communication at a clean, non-staged property or yard. |
| Contact | A crew member speaking with a homeowner after assessment or completion. | Keep body language relaxed and helpful; leave uncluttered space for the contact-page message. |
| Gallery | A broad, perfectly cleaned property after professional work, with fresh-cut logs stacked neatly. | Healthy trees, a cleaned lawn, neatly stacked logs, and organized equipment support the idea of thorough cleanup. |
| Future city pages | Unique regional property/service context for the page. | Change property type, tree species, season, camera angle, and activity for every city. Never present generic imagery as city-specific proof. |

## Supporting and Gallery Images

Supporting images may be more tightly framed than heroes, but should still
belong to the same campaign. They should clarify a benefit, process step, or
result. Use variety deliberately:

- Detail: hands organizing rigging or inspecting a healthy branch.
- Process: a crew staging a safe work zone or loading debris.
- Result: a clean yard, restored driveway access, tidy mulch, or healthy canopy.
- People: a short, natural consultation or a crew coordinating work.

Avoid generic posed handshakes, isolated tool closeups with no context, smiling
workers looking directly at the camera, and repetitive rows of nearly identical
tree-removal scenes.

## Prompt Construction Standard

Prompts must state the business purpose, subject, setting, lighting,
composition, safety details, and exclusions. Begin with a concrete scene and
write the text-safe area intentionally.

Use this template:

```text
Photorealistic editorial service photograph for Metro East Tree Pros.
[Specific service or company moment] at a believable modern Metro East Illinois
[residential/commercial] property. [Crew count/role] in coordinated deep-green
work shirts, practical workwear, and appropriate white hard hats/PPE. Show
[realistic equipment and safe work method]. Bright natural [summer/early-fall]
daylight, natural Midwestern trees and landscaping, premium but documentary
feel. Wide 16:9 landscape composition, [left/right/center] 40% clean negative
space for website headline and buttons; place the action on the opposite side.
No text, watermark, phone number, oversized logos, prominent vehicle branding,
stock-photo posing, unsafe work practices, distorted anatomy, or unrealistic
equipment.
```

For every prompt, specify what makes the composition unique from all current
heroes: camera angle, subject placement, property type, season, tree species,
crew action, and background. Do not use vague phrases such as “make it look
professional” in place of concrete direction.

## Required Review Before Use

Before moving a generated asset into `public/images` or assigning it to a page,
complete this review:

- [ ] The composition is materially distinct from every existing major-page
      hero.
- [ ] The location, house, crew pose, truck angle, and tree are not a
      near-duplicate of another assigned hero.
- [ ] The service depicted matches the destination page.
- [ ] Safety gear, rope work, equipment, body positions, and work zones appear
      credible.
- [ ] The image has no text, watermark, broken pseudo-logo, malformed hands,
      duplicated body parts, or visually implausible objects.
- [ ] The desktop and mobile crops retain an understandable subject and leave
      room for the site’s existing overlay and headings.
- [ ] The color, contrast, lighting, and cleanliness match the campaign.
- [ ] The filename is descriptive, lowercase, and kebab-case.
- [ ] The asset is optimized for web delivery and the page uses `next/image`
      with meaningful alt text and appropriate `sizes`.
- [ ] The asset is not presented as verified customer work, an actual employee,
      or proof of city-level service without business approval.

## Alt Text and Editorial Honesty

Alt text should describe the visible action succinctly. It should not repeat a
heading, claim a city or job is real, or load location keywords into the
description.

Good: “Tree-service crew lowering a cut section from a mature tree beside a
residential yard.”

Good: “Crew member discussing tree-service options with a homeowner in a clean
yard.”

Avoid: “Best Belleville tree removal company at a completed customer project.”

If a generated visual is used in a gallery or alongside copy that could imply
documentary proof, label it as a representative visual until genuine approved
project photography replaces it. Never use artificial visuals to create false
reviews, testimonials, certifications, or before-and-after claims.

## Delivery and Lifecycle

Save approved web assets in the purpose-based paths established in
[IMAGE_STRATEGY.md](./IMAGE_STRATEGY.md), such as:

```text
public/images/home/home-hero.jpg
public/images/services/tree-removal-hero.jpg
public/images/about/about-hero.jpg
public/images/locations/belleville-hero.jpg
```

Use descriptive lowercase kebab-case names. Optimize the web derivative before
commit; aim for a high-quality hero under roughly 500 KB where practical, while
preserving the tree, equipment, and facial detail needed for credibility. Keep
the original master only where the project’s asset process permits it.

When an image is missing, retain the existing branded gradient fallback. Never
substitute another page’s hero just to fill the space. Record the missing asset
in [IMAGE_STRATEGY.md](./IMAGE_STRATEGY.md) and update the page only after the
new visual passes this guide’s review.

## Final Decision Rule

Choose the image that most clearly communicates safe, professional, local
service while making the page easier to read and trust. If a visual is
impressive but questionable, repetitive, unsafe, misleading, or difficult to
crop on mobile, reject it and generate a better one.
